# Safety guidelines

## General safety

- Respect privacy. Do not request medical records or unnecessary personal information.
- Do not expose, store, or log API keys.
- Preserve uncertainty and require toy confirmation before transformation guidance.
- Stop if a part resists unexpectedly. Do not use excessive force.
- Working with another person is encouraged. The app is an aid, not a definitive or guaranteed transformation guide.
- Do not imitate or reproduce a real person's voice.

## Accessible instructions

- Keep the app usable with keyboard navigation and screen readers.
- Do not send a blind or low-vision user back to an image as the explanation. Do not use “as shown,” “as indicated,” “follow the arrows,” or similar phrases.
- Do not refer to an unseen image, inset, panel, numbered box, arrow, or depiction. Translate visual evidence into physical language.
- Every step must independently identify its current viewpoint—front, rear, side, top-down, underside, or uncertain—and compare it with the immediately preceding step. If both views are top-down, do not describe one as front-facing.
- Identify the part, location, connection point, fixed pivot, axis, direction, endpoint, surface, alignment, and rotation when supported.
- Stop rather than invent a movement when the source images do not support one.

## Source and interpretation safety

- Keep TFWiki entry evidence attached to the specific product it supports. A heading or explicit release statement may establish toyline or subline context; a similar product name in another heading must not be substituted.
- Treat character identity, fictional property, parent toyline, subline, and size class as separate concepts.
- Treat weapons and other accessories as separate tracked objects. A piece leaving a hand or body may be an accessory and must not be called missing without evidence.
- Portkey may inspect TFWiki, Unicron, and instruction imagery for supported warnings about fragile parts, breakage, degrading plastics, rubber or other textures, paint or clear parts, liquid features, small-parts hazards, and force.
- Distinguish source-supported warnings from visual observations. Do not invent a hazard or claim a material is present without support.
- Keep external interpretation calls bounded to prevent loops and unnecessary spending.

## Scope and collaboration

- Generated instructions may not take a blind or low-vision user successfully from one mode to another when followed alone.
- The output should provide useful starting language for a sighted or non-sighted friend or family member to coach the transformation.
- Collaboration is part of play; the app should support shared problem-solving rather than imply that the user must work alone.
- A full launch should involve Hasbro and established Transformers fan-resource maintainers regarding permissions, attribution, stable source access, preservation, and accessibility review.
